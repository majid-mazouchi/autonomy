05 · Bias Drills

Catch your brain in the act

Behavioral biases are not knowledge gaps, they are wiring. You cannot delete them, but you can learn to feel them firing. Answer honestly; the point is to be caught.

Loss aversion

Losses hurt roughly twice as much as equal gains feel good. It makes you gamble to avoid losses and play too safe with gains.

Anchoring

The first number you saw (an old high, your buy price, an IPO price) drags every later judgment toward it, though the market owes it nothing.

Disposition effect

The urge to sell winners quickly and cling to losers. It systematically prunes your good decisions and waters your bad ones.

Confirmation bias

Once you own a stock you start collecting only agreeing evidence. Deliberately read the bear case for everything you hold.

06 · The Trade Journal

The tool that actually improves traders

Every serious trader keeps a journal. Not for the numbers, for the honesty: why you entered, how you felt, what you would repeat. Fill this in after any trade, real or paper, and copy the entry into your notes app.

Your formatted journal entry appears here. Screenshot it or copy it into your notes.
Rule of thumb. If the feeling box says FOMO, revenge, or bored more than once a week, the journal has found your real problem, and it is not the charts.
07 · Habits

A practice routine that compounds

1

Paper trade with rules, not vibes

Same position sizing, same stops, same journal as real money. Otherwise it teaches bad habits.

2

Review weekly

Reread your journal every weekend. Patterns in your behavior appear fast.

3

One setup at a time

Master a single kind of trade before adding another. Collectors of setups master none.

4

Graduate slowly

Only move to real money after weeks of disciplined paper profit, and then start tiny.
